# Changelog

## Unreleased

Work toward v0.2.0.

- **PDFs are checked before they are read.** PDF to TXT now runs every file through a guard first, which measures compressed data and the sizes readers allocate from without trusting what the file declares, and refuses files built to make a reader use gigabytes of memory (decompression bombs in content, fonts, pictures and object streams, through Flate, LZW, RunLength, ASCII85 and ASCIIHex, chained and with predictors; pictures, sampled functions and rows of absurd size; too many objects or pages; Brotli streams). Measured on the previous build, such files used up to 4 GB before failing with a misleading message. Whether a page is a scan is now decided without decoding its pictures.
- **Encrypted PDFs are not opened**, including ones that open without a password but restrict copying or printing; until now their text could be extracted. FileConvert never removes a PDF's protection.
- **Merge PDFs**, a new tool with its own page. PDFs in the order you set become one: every page copied as it is (nothing is redrawn or recompressed), with its links, annotations and form fields; links, bookmarks and named destinations point at the right pages of the merged file. Bookmarks are rebuilt as one per file, named after it, with the file's own bookmarks under it, open or closed as they were. A form field, named destination or form font whose name an earlier file already uses is renamed (name_2 in the second file), so nothing is overwritten, and the default appearance of each file's fields is kept. Layers keep their settings. The first file's title, author and other document information are kept. Each PDF is checked as it is added (pages, a digital signature, the PDF guard); encrypted PDFs are refused, and a signed PDF has to be accepted before merging, since its signature will no longer be valid. Accessibility tags, page labels, files attached to the document, document scripts, article threads and XFA form data are not carried, and the result lists what each file loses. Up to 200 files, 5,000 pages and 1 GB together, less on a device that reports little memory.
- **Split a PDF, Extract pages, Delete pages, Reorder pages and Rotate pages**, five new tools with their own pages, on the same checks as Merge PDFs. Split by page ranges (overlapping allowed), every few pages, or into single pages, with names that say the pages (report-p04-07.pdf) and a ZIP for many; split and extract copy only the pages taken, so nothing of the others is in a new file, and keep the links, bookmarks, named destinations and form fields that belong to their pages. Delete leaves nothing of the deleted pages in the file and never deletes every page. Reorder by dragging, with Up and Down, or by typing the order; links and bookmarks follow their pages. Rotate a quarter turn either way or upside down, every page or some, adding to the rotation a page already has. Page lists are typed as 1-3, 7, 10-, and mistakes are refused with what to write instead.
- A form's calculation order no longer keeps a field of a removed page, and its value, in the file.
- Removing pages from a PDF no longer counts the entries hidden under a closed bookmark as shown, and no longer takes a form field's widget or a note's popup that has text and a parent for a stray page.

- **Pages for common conversions.** HEIC to JPG, MP4 to MP3, JSON to CSV and 19 more have their own page, such as `/fileconvert/heic-to-jpg/`, with the converter at the top (it chooses that output for every file you add), what happens to your files, what to expect, and related conversions. The text is in the page itself, so it reads without scripts.
- **Find a conversion.** Search every page as you type and filter by category. Asking for a conversion without its own page (BMP to TIFF) says whether FileConvert does it; asking for a format it does not support (AVI) says why.
- **Common conversions** are listed under the drop zone on the home page.
- The promise is now "Convert files. Upload nothing."
- The layout works on screens 320 pixels wide.
- A tab left open while FileConvert is updated, which then asks for a converter the new version no longer has, now says to reload the page instead of blaming memory.
- **Excel to JSON and JSON to Excel.** JSON from a workbook keeps numbers, true/false and empty cells as JSON values and writes dates as ISO 8601 text; pick one sheet or get all of them in one file. JSON to Excel gives typed cells, one sheet per list, text that can never run as a formula, and every digit of large integers. New pages: Excel to JSON and JSON to Excel.
- **TSV is its own format**, read and written, to and from CSV, JSON and Excel, with the same formula protection as CSV. Converted files say how many cells protection changed, and it can be turned off.
- **AIFF and AIFF-C** audio converts to every audio format: 8 to 32-bit, little-endian, float, mu-law and A-law, read in pieces however large; 16-bit AIFF to WAV or FLAC keeps every sample. Compressed AIFF-C is refused with its codec named. New page: AIFF to MP3.
- **MPEG-TS** (.ts, .mts, .m2ts) converts like other video. Its stream table is read, so what cannot be converted is named instead of dropped: MPEG-2 video (TV recordings) offers only its sound, and sound in a private stream (AVCHD camcorders) is reported. New page: MPEG-TS to MP4.
- Video whose sound this browser cannot decode (AC-3 outside Edge, for example) now converts without sound, as the note on the file says; it used to stop with an error unless Remove the sound was on.
- Video and audio lengths are measured from the first timestamp, so files that do not start at zero show their real length.
- **Animation is kept.** Animated GIF, WebP and PNG (APNG) convert to animated GIF, animated WebP, MP4 or WebM with every frame, its timing, the loop count, transparency, and the disposal and blending rules of the source. The output menu marks which formats keep the animation and which keep only the first frame, and says so before converting. New pages: GIF to WebP and WebP to GIF.
- When a browser cannot encode H.264 at a size, an animation converted to MP4 now says it holds VP9 instead.
- **SVG to PNG, JPG, WebP and the other picture formats**, drawn at its own size, two or four times larger, or a width up to 4096 pixels. Every SVG is cleaned first: scripts, event handlers, HTML content, animation and every link to a file, font or stylesheet outside the SVG are removed, entity declarations are refused, and nothing in the file runs or is downloaded. New pages: SVG to PNG and SVG to JPG.
- **Remove photo metadata**, a new privacy tool with its own page. It takes EXIF camera details, dates, GPS location, maker notes, embedded thumbnails, XMP, IPTC and Photoshop data, comments, PNG text fields and extra data after the picture (such as a motion photo's video) out of JPG, PNG and WebP photos, without recompressing them: the picture data is copied byte for byte. Orientation (as a minimal EXIF block holding nothing else) and the color profile are kept unless you turn them off. Each photo says what was removed and kept and its size before and after; batches download as a ZIP.
- Tools have their own pages, listed under Tools on the home page, and run in a shared workspace with ordering, options, progress, cancel and ZIP downloads.
- **Combine images into a TIFF**, a new tool: pictures in the order you set become the pages of one multi-page TIFF, each turned upright, uncompressed or LZW; a multi-page TIFF among them adds all its pages.
- **ICO in and out.** Icons from any picture: a favicon with 16, 32 and 48 pixel sizes, all six sizes up to 256, or one size, each kept transparent. ICO and CUR files are read too, whether their pictures are PNG or 32-bit, 8-bit or 1-bit bitmaps with a transparency mask. New page: PNG to ICO.
- **Multi-page TIFF.** Every page of a multi-page TIFF converts (one file per page, or one TIFF keeping them all in order), or only the first page if you choose. TIFF can be written with LZW compression, which is lossless and read by Windows, macOS and Office.
- **Unpack ZIP, TAR and GZ archives**, .tar.gz and .tgz included. An archive gets Extract files instead of a list of outputs; its files are listed with what each one is, to download one by one, or to add to the list and convert without adding them again. Archives are never converted into another archive format. Limits apply to what is actually unpacked, not to what the archive claims: a file that unpacks to more than its listed size or expands more than 1000 times is stopped, and the number of files (10,000) and the total unpacked size (4 GB) are capped. Paths that climb out of the archive, absolute paths and drive letters are shown without those parts and never used; links, devices, encrypted files and unsupported compression methods are listed with the reason; an archive inside an archive is unpacked only when you ask; ZIP and gzip checksums are verified. Unpacked files are kept in the browser's private storage and deleted once nothing in the list needs them. RAR and 7-Zip are not supported, and search says so.
- Emoji and other characters beyond the Basic Multilingual Plane are kept in workbooks written by Python tools such as pandas; they used to become a different character on the way to CSV.
- Mediabunny and its encoders are updated to 1.66.2.
- **Extract files from a ZIP**, and **Extract TAR, TAR.GZ and GZ files**, have pages of their own. They are the converter itself, with the archive's formats explained.
- **Markdown and Word.** HTML to Markdown, DOCX to HTML, DOCX to Markdown and Markdown to DOCX. Markdown output is GitHub-flavored, with tables, fenced code with its language, nested lists and footnotes; DOCX to HTML is one standalone page that loads nothing; Markdown to DOCX uses Word's own heading, list, link and table styles, so the navigation pane and a table of contents work. Code and quote styles from Word, LibreOffice and pandoc, and text set in a code font, become code and quotes. Everything is sanitized first, a document cannot choose its own HTML, and nothing is fetched: pictures a DOCX only links to are left out with a note, and pictures from the web stay addresses or become their description. These conversions keep the structure, not the look, and say what they leave out. A new Documents setting chooses whether pictures stay inside the Markdown. New pages: HTML to Markdown, DOCX to HTML, DOCX to Markdown and Markdown to DOCX.
- **HTML to DOCX**, with its own page. The page is cleaned as for every other output and written in Word's own styles: headings, real Word lists that keep their starting number, tables with their header row, merged cells and tables inside cells, links (links within the page become jumps to the heading they name), code, quotes, underline, highlighting, subscript and superscript, and the PNG, JPEG, GIF and BMP pictures inside the file. Styles are not applied, and pictures from the web or a relative address become their description; nothing is downloaded.
- **Markdown with its pictures as separate files.** A third choice for pictures in Markdown (Documents settings) gives one ZIP for DOCX or HTML to Markdown: the `.md` file with an `assets` folder beside it, picture names that need no escaping, a picture used twice stored once, and SVG pictures cleaned before they are written. The links are relative, so they work wherever the ZIP is extracted, a GitHub repository included. Keeping pictures inside the Markdown is still the default, and a document with no pictures inside it still gives the `.md` alone.
- **XLS and ODS workbooks** convert to XLSX (every sheet, value, formula with its saved result, number format and merged range, and for XLS the column widths and hidden sheets), CSV, TSV, JSON and JSON Lines, with the sheet choice XLSX has. What is not kept (cell formatting, charts, pictures, comments, macros) is listed. New pages: XLS to XLSX, XLS to CSV, ODS to XLSX and ODS to CSV.
- **JSON Lines** (`.jsonl`, `.ndjson`, and NDJSON saved as `.json`, recognized by its lines) to and from JSON, CSV, TSV and Excel. A line that is not valid stops the conversion and is named rather than skipped. New pages: JSON Lines to CSV and JSON to JSON Lines.
- **XML and YAML to CSV, TSV and Excel**, by written rules (README, How data becomes a table): XML records are the elements that repeat, one table each; JSON and YAML lists of records are tables; what sits beside the records is repeated on every row instead of being dropped. JSON to CSV and Excel follow the same rules, so an object holding a list of records beside other fields now gives one row per record instead of a single row. Two lists of records give two tables, never a combination of their rows; lists of records inside a row stay in that row; a list that mixes records with other values is kept whole; and the notes explain each of these. New pages: XML to CSV, XML to XLSX and YAML to CSV.
- JSON, JSON Lines and YAML to CSV and Excel no longer lose a value when a key with a dot in its name (`a.b`) sits beside a nested field of the same dotted name: the later one is `a.b_2`. Empty lists and objects are written `[]` and `{}` with either list setting (numbered columns used to leave them empty), a top-level list beside the records follows the list setting (it was always spread into numbered columns), and an empty list, an empty object or records without fields give an empty file with a note instead of blank lines. A key repeated inside one JSON object, which every JSON reader resolves to its last value, is now counted in a note instead of passing silently.
- Spreadsheet to CSV with values as shown no longer cuts numbers in the General format to 11 characters (123456789012345 used to become 1.23457E+14, and 1234.5678901234 became 1234.56789); they keep the 15 digits Excel stores.

## v0.1.0 - 2026-11-15

The first release.

### Conversions

- **Images:** JPG, PNG, WebP, AVIF, HEIC/HEIF, BMP, GIF and TIFF to JPG, PNG, WebP, AVIF, GIF, BMP and TIFF, with quality, resizing (by percent or to fit a box), transparency kept or replaced by a chosen color, and EXIF orientation applied. Animated GIF to animated GIF, MP4 or WebM.
- **Audio:** MP3, WAV, FLAC, AAC, M4A and OGG to MP3, WAV, FLAC, AAC, M4A and OGG (Opus), with bitrate, sample rate and channels, and the soundtrack of any supported video.
- **Video:** MP4, WebM, MOV and MKV to MP4, WebM, MOV, MKV and GIF, with resolution, quality and removing the sound. Streams are copied without re-encoding when the new container can hold them.
- **Documents:** TXT to PDF, PDF to TXT, Markdown to HTML and PDF, HTML to PDF, and DOCX to TXT.
- **Data:** JSON to CSV, YAML and XML; CSV to JSON and XLSX; YAML to JSON; XML to JSON; XLSX to CSV.

### Interface

- Drag and drop or a file picker, many files at once, format detection from file contents with a note when the name is wrong, outputs offered per file, Set all to, per-category settings, a queue with real progress, cancel, Convert all, single downloads and a batch ZIP, numbered names when results share a name.
- Dark and light themes, keyboard use throughout, a layout that works from 360 pixels wide, Share, and Back to RadMage Labs.

### Privacy and security

- Every conversion runs in the browser; nothing is uploaded, and the Content Security Policy allows connections only to the site itself.
- Content-based detection, decompression bomb checks for images and ZIP-based documents, refusal of XML entity declarations and YAML alias bombs, nesting limits, HTML sanitizing, spreadsheet formula protection in CSV, safe file names, and size limits that keep the browser from running out of memory.
- Pictures lose their EXIF metadata. Audio and video keep descriptive tags but not raw container metadata.
